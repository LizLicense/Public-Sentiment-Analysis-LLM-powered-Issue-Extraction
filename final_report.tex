\begin{abstract}
Singapore telecommunications operators receive continuous, unsolicited customer feedback through online forums, but the volume, informality and platform-specific language make manual monitoring difficult. This project develops an end-to-end pipeline that collects forum discussions, normalises heterogeneous records, identifies telecommunications brands, assigns sentiment and issue labels, and presents findings through a static HTML dashboard. The analysis corpus combines Reddit and HardwareZone (HWZ) posts up to 26~September~2026: after removing repeated copies and off-topic threads, 9,162 unique posts in 349 telco threads remain. GPT-4o-mini labels 44\% of them not happy and 19\% happy (42\% and 18\% with threads weighted equally), whereas VADER on the same posts reports 28\% and 46\%. Network quality is the largest issue theme (37--41\% of posts), followed by mobile plans (13--14\%) and pricing (7--8\%). Reddit and HWZ differ mainly in topic rather than tone, and no brand stands out beyond small, weighting-sensitive differences. The original, smaller corpus (1,711 posts) serves as a case study: 39\% of it sat in threads about one March 2026 Singtel outage, which inflated negativity to 55\% and made Singtel and Reddit look uniquely negative.

The system combines deterministic brand matching, VADER sentiment, a keyword issue taxonomy, an optional Hugging Face sentiment model and OpenAI chat-completions classification. Independent human labels on a blinded 300-row representative sample (295 telco-relevant rows) show that \textbf{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18} improves sentiment over VADER (macro F1 0.631 versus 0.360; accuracy 67\% versus 37\%). \textbf{gpt-4o-2024-08-06} is similar on sentiment (macro F1 0.623) and still loses to keyword issue rules when it labels every row (macro F1 0.600 versus 0.639). A hybrid that keeps rule labels and applies GPT-4o only to unresolved \texttt{general\_discussion} rows raises issue macro F1 to \textbf{0.680}, and was applied to 8,786 unresolved corpus rows (29.7 minutes, estimated \textbf{\$3.45}). On a held-out 100-post challenge set of harder posts, the sentiment result holds (GPT-4o-mini 0.773 versus VADER 0.491), but the hybrid's gain over rules is not significant (0.509 versus 0.499) and GPT-4o labelling every post scores best (0.596), so no single issue method wins on both sets. GPT-4o-mini sentiment on all 9,162 posts cost about \$0.28, and a repeated run reproduced 292 of 295 gold-set labels. All gold labels come from the original corpus, so accuracy on the newer posts is assumed until a wave-2 gold sample is labelled. An earlier 150-record VADER ``100\% accuracy'' file is discarded because its labels match VADER exactly and were model-prefilled. The operational recommendation is therefore LLM sentiment (mini is sufficient), keyword rules plus a targeted GPT-4o pass for issues, and human review of alerts. The business analysis indicates that operators should prioritise incident communication during outages, comparable plan terms such as fair-use limits and add-on charges, and equipment and app friction, with billing and customer-service aftercare planned for incidents; roaming and porting are small in this corpus. The principal contribution is a reproducible analytics workflow that converts noisy forum data into decision-oriented signals while making its evidential limits explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Business context}

Mobile customers discuss price plans, coverage, roaming, porting and service failures in public online communities. These discussions can reveal emerging pain points earlier and in more detail than periodic surveys. They are also difficult to analyse reliably: posts contain Singlish, abbreviations, sarcasm, quoted text, multiple brands and off-topic uses of ambiguous brand names such as ``Eight'' or ``M1''.

This project asks how a telecommunications operator can convert such unstructured discussion into a repeatable source of customer insight. The objective is not to infer population-level satisfaction or replace operational metrics. Instead, the pipeline is designed as an early-warning and exploratory decision-support system that helps analysts locate themes, compare discussion contexts and inspect representative records.

\subsection{Objectives}

The project has five objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item collect relevant discussions from multiple online platforms;
  \item clean and standardise heterogeneous post and comment records;
  \item detect mentioned operators and classify sentiment and service issues;
  \item compare lightweight baselines with more context-aware model options; and
  \item translate the results into actionable recommendations through a dashboard.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research questions}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0cm,style=nextline]
  \item[RQ1.] What sentiment and issue themes appear most frequently in the collected Singapore telco discussions?
  \item[RQ2.] How do discussion patterns differ between Reddit and HWZ, and across telco brands?
  \item[RQ3.] What additional value can a context-aware model provide over VADER sentiment and keyword-based issue rules?
  \item[RQ4.] How can the extracted signals support concrete decisions by telco operators?
\end{description}

\subsection{Scope and claimed contribution}

The repository implements collection modules for HWZ and Reddit. It includes preprocessing, brand detection, sentiment labelling, issue extraction, evaluation scripts and a static HTML dashboard. The report only claims findings supported by persisted repository outputs. GPT-4o-mini and GPT-4o are evaluated on human labels for both the representative and challenge sets, and GPT-4o is used on unresolved corpus issues. The findings and dashboard use GPT-4o-mini sentiment and hybrid issue labels on the 9,162-post v2 corpus; the original 1,711-post corpus is kept as an outage case study.

The scraped rows are not independent posts. The original 15,504 rows hold 2,693 unique posts, because successive scrape rounds saved the same comments again; rounds 2--9 of ten added no new posts. Forty-seven of 152 threads were off-topic, such as a 98-post insurance lawsuit thread, and matched a keyword by accident. \texttt{processing/build\_clean\_corpus.py} keeps one row per post ID (Reddit comment or post ID; HWZ thread URL and timestamp), so identical text from different users stays separate, and drops a thread unless at least 20\% of its posts name a telco or its title is telco-related; kept threads name a telco in at least 25\% of their posts and dropped threads in at most 8\%. The original corpus has 1,711 posts in 105 threads (1,257 Reddit, 454 HWZ). Combining it with wave~2 and applying the same steps gives the v2 corpus: 24,690 rows, 11,643 unique posts, 96 off-topic threads dropped, and 9,162 posts in 349 threads (5,711 Reddit, 3,451 HWZ). An earlier 158-post dashboard sample (67 Reddit, 91 HWZ) is superseded.

\subsection{Preprocessing}

The preprocessing module maps platform-specific fields to a common schema, removes empty, placeholder and very short records, and can optionally retain English-language records using language detection. The common schema includes \texttt{source}, \texttt{thread\_title}, \texttt{post\_text}, timestamp fields and identifiers. Normalisation allows downstream modules to process different platforms without source-specific model code.

The current workflow retains post text that may include informal spelling, emoji and HTML entities because these features can carry sentiment. This is useful for analysis but also creates modelling challenges. The dashboard therefore presents aggregate indicators and traceable examples rather than treating model output as verified customer facts.

\subsection{End-to-end architecture}

The implemented sequence is:

\section{Analytical Methods}

\subsection{Telco detection}

Brand mentions are identified by regular expressions over the concatenated thread title and post text. The tracked labels include Singtel, StarHub, M1, SIMBA, Giga, Eight, Zym and redONE, with additional MVNO keywords used by the dashboard. Multiple brands can be assigned to one record. This design is transparent and inexpensive, but ambiguous names and thread-level context can create false positives. For example, a post containing the ordinary word ``eight'' may be incorrectly associated with the Eight brand unless the surrounding context is considered.

\subsection{Sentiment baseline: VADER}

VADER is a lexicon- and rule-based sentiment method designed for social-media text \cite{hutto2014vader}. For each post, the pipeline obtains a compound score and maps it to:

\begin{equation}
\text{label}(s)=
\begin{cases}
\text{happy}, & s \geq 0.05,\\
\text{not\_happy}, & s \leq -0.05,\\
\text{neutral}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

VADER is a suitable operational baseline because it runs locally, is deterministic and requires no paid API. Its limitation is contextual understanding. It can misread sarcasm, negation over long passages, mixed opinions, quoted complaints and the difference between sentiment towards a telco and sentiment towards another topic in the same thread.

\subsection{Context-aware sentiment option}

The benchmark module supports \texttt{cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest}, a RoBERTa-family model adapted to social-media sentiment \cite{loureiro2022tweeteval}. Its positive, negative and neutral outputs are mapped to the project's three business labels. The persisted benchmark states that the required \texttt{transformers} package was unavailable, so no transformer metrics are reported.

The main labelling module also implements LLM sentiment through an OpenAI-compatible chat-completions interface. Its configured default is \textbf{GPT-4o-mini}. Formal comparison uses the snapshots returned by the API, \textbf{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18} and \textbf{gpt-4o-2024-08-06}, with temperature 0 and JSON object responses in batches of ten posts. An earlier 100-record HWZ file contains 100 neutral LLM labels and no model-version metadata; that file is treated as a failed or fallback run and is not used as evidence. Both corpora were labelled with the same prompt, batching and settings through \texttt{models/corpus\_llm\_sentiment.py}; the checkpoint is keyed by post ID, so posts shared by the two corpora were labelled once. The original corpus cost \$0.048 and the full v2 corpus about \$0.28.

On the representative gold set that hybrid beats both keyword-only and all-row LLM tagging, so it was run on the 8,786 unresolved corpus rows. Section~\ref{sec:challenge} tests it on held-out posts.

\section{Evaluation Design and Evidence}

\subsection{Required metrics}

For a three-class sentiment task, overall accuracy alone can hide poor performance on minority classes. The evaluation module therefore calculates a confusion matrix and per-class precision, recall and F1, together with macro F1. For issue extraction, the appropriate measures are per-class precision, recall and F1 under a multi-label protocol, plus micro and macro averages.

Model comparison uses the same independently annotated records. Runtime is measured on the same machine, and the API run records input tokens, output tokens, retries, the exact returned model identifier and estimated cost from the provider list prices supplied at run time.

\subsection{Independent annotation}

A blinded evaluation package was generated without VADER, transformer or LLM columns. Threads were split so that representative and challenge posts do not share threads, with at most five comments per thread. Annotator~A labelled:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a 300-row representative sheet (214 Reddit, 86 HWZ), of which 295 posts were telco-relevant;
  \item a 100-row challenge sheet reserved for harder cases (negation, sarcasm or Singlish cues, mixed brands, weak VADER scores, long posts).
\end{itemize}

A second annotator labelled a 100-row overlap (60 representative, 40 challenge; \texttt{double\_annotation\_annotator\_b\_filled\_guide.csv}). On the representative overlap, sentiment agreement is 60\% (Cohen's $\kappa=0.38$ \cite{cohen1960kappa}, $n=58$), which is fair agreement at best; on the challenge overlap it is 75\% ($\kappa=0.63$, $n=40$). Most disagreements share one pattern: posts Annotator~A labelled \texttt{not\_happy} that Annotator~B labelled \texttt{neutral} (12 of 23 disagreements on the representative overlap, 8 of 10 on the challenge overlap). They are bare reports of a failure (``Ya Singtel is down''), questions about compensation, or troubleshooting advice. The annotation guide listed ``service failure'' under \texttt{not\_happy} and ``factual statement'' under \texttt{neutral} without saying which takes precedence, and such posts are both, so the two annotators resolved them differently. The guide now states that a first-hand failure report is \texttt{not\_happy}, which is Annotator~A's reading; the overlap labels predate that rule, and model scores against Annotator~A partly reflect it. Two further cautions apply. An earlier version of Annotator~B's sheet is superseded, because its sentiment labels often contradicted B's own notes and matched Annotator~A instead. And on the challenge overlap, 18 of 40 rows still match Annotator~A on every field, including four identical free-text notes, so the challenge $\kappa$ is not relied on until B's independence on those rows is confirmed. Headline metrics use Annotator~A's representative labels; challenge metrics are reported separately and are not pooled.

An earlier 150-row file reported 100\% VADER accuracy. Those labels match VADER exactly and can be created by prefilling from the model, so that file is treated as leakage and is not used as evidence.

\subsection{Held-out challenge set ($n=100$)}
\label{sec:challenge}

The challenge set was sampled from threads not used in the representative set and weighted toward hard cases (negation, sarcasm, Singlish, mixed brands, long posts). It is reported separately and never pooled. Because the hybrid was chosen after seeing representative-set results, the challenge set is its held-out test. \section{Findings}

RQ3 is answered in Section~4: GPT-4o-mini adds sentiment context over VADER on typical and hard posts; for issues, rules plus GPT-4o on unresolved posts is best on typical posts, and GPT-4o labelling every post is best on hard ones. This section applies GPT-4o-mini sentiment and hybrid issue labels to the v2 corpus: 9,162 unique posts in 349 telco threads, combining the original data with wave-2 posts from 10~April to 26~September~2026. Shares are reported per post and with every thread weighted equally, so that one large thread cannot drive a finding. Section~\ref{sec:casestudy} then reads the original 1,711-post corpus as a case study of one outage.

\subsection{Case study: the March 2026 Singtel outage}
\label{sec:casestudy}

The original corpus (1,711 unique posts, collected up to 9~April~2026) is dominated by one event: 22 outage threads hold 665 posts (39\%), led by ``16 March 26: Singtel mobile service outage''. It shows how an incident distorts every aggregate. In that corpus 55\% of posts are not happy, but 42\% outside the outage threads; Singtel is 63\% not happy but 44\% outside them, level with StarHub (47\%); Reddit is 60\% not happy against HWZ 40\%, but 43\% against 40\% outside them, because every original Reddit post is from 2026 and Reddit holds the outage threads. Billing and customer service were the most negative themes there, and many of those posts were outage aftercare: whether compensation is a refund or a goodwill credit, and support lines that could not be reached.

The weekly huddle view catches the event. In the original corpus, three weeks cross the 20-post threshold, all in March--April 2026 (352, 70 and 42 queue posts). On v2, with a threshold set relative to a normal week (Section~\ref{sec:dashboard}), only two weeks qualify: the outage week (352 posts) and the week of 14~September~2026 (149 posts). The second is a different kind of event, spread across brands: StarHub broadband going down, OTP failures on the SIMBA app, and complaints in the SIMBA, Singtel, M1 Maxx and Eight threads. The screen therefore flags both a single-operator outage and a multi-brand bad week, which is the early-warning use the project set out to support.

\subsection{Dashboard design}
\label{sec:dashboard}

\texttt{dashboard/generate\_dashboard.py} builds a static page from a clean corpus: \texttt{index\_v2.html} for v2 and \texttt{index\_v1.html} for the original corpus. Chart titles are computed from the data and state the takeaway (for example, ``Network Quality leads, then Mobile Plans''). Where a result would invite a wrong reading, the title says so: a per-post Reddit--HWZ gap is shown as ``check topic mix first'', not as a platform effect. The page opens with a weekly 30-minute huddle playbook and a weekly queue chart: (a) not-happy posts in Network Quality, App \& Account or Porting; (b) open ten source posts; (c) match them to tickets or outages; (d) assign one owner; (e) check next week whether the share fell. A fixed threshold does not scale with the corpus: 20 posts suited the original data but is crossed in 27 of the v2 weeks. The v2 page therefore acts when a week reaches twice the median of the last 26 weeks (115 posts).

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tone:} GPT-4o-mini shares beside VADER and the human gold labels, so readers see why VADER is not used.
  \item \textbf{Source:} Reddit and HWZ on the same labeller, with the gold-label comparison and the topic-mix caveat.
  \item \textbf{Issue volume:} per-post and thread-weighted shares side by side; a finding that holds in both is not one thread talking.
  \item \textbf{Not-happy by issue:} which themes carry the negative discussion.
  \item \textbf{Brands:} ranked only with at least 1\% of posts and 10 threads, with thread counts in the table.
  \item \textbf{Recommendations:} ordered by each issue's share of not-happy discussion.
\end{itemize}

Outputs remain screening signals, not verified customer facts.

\section{Business Recommendations}

Each recommendation names an owner, an action and a check. They are ordered by each issue's share of not-happy discussion in the v2 corpus. Forum counts are a triage queue, not proof of market failure.

\subsection{Network: communicate during incidents, then prove local fixes}

Network quality carries 46\% of not-happy discussion. Of 1,991 not-happy network posts, 621 sit in outage threads, and the huddle flagged both the March outage and the September StarHub broadband disruption. The first action is incident communication: a status page and proactive updates with an estimated restoration time, posted where customers are already asking. Outside outages, 289 not-happy network posts name a place such as MRT tunnels, homes or offices; network teams should join those posts to tickets and cell data. Success is a shorter time to the first public update during an incident, and fewer tickets at a named location after remediation.

\subsection{Product and commercial: make plan terms comparable}

Mobile plans (13\% of posts, in 202 threads) and pricing (8\%, 42\% not happy) are the second and third sources of not-happy discussion. Posts centre on terms customers discover late: fair-use limits on ``unlimited'' plans, add-on charges such as Caller ID (which posts note the three network operators charge for while SIMBA includes it), introductory prices that later change, and which host network an MVNO uses. Product and commercial teams should show fair-use rules, add-on charges, recurring price and host network at checkout. Success is fewer ``which plan?'' and hidden-charge threads.

\subsection{Devices and digital: fix equipment and app friction}

Device posts (6\%) cover eSIM failures and ONR/bridge-mode router policy; device support should publish router and eSIM requirements per plan. App and account problems are small per post but spread across 115 threads, including OTP failures on the SIMBA app in the September spike week; digital teams should watch forum posts after each app release. Success is fewer equipment threads and fewer ``cannot log in'' posts after a release.

\subsection{Customer service and billing: plan the incident aftercare}

Customer service (59\% not happy) and billing (53\%) are small but the most negative themes, and in the outage case study they were largely aftercare. Service and billing teams should publish the compensation rule with the incident notice and keep a self-service path open during outages. Success is fewer compensation and ``cannot reach support'' posts after the next incident.

\subsection{Lower-volume themes: roaming and porting}

Roaming (4\%) and porting (3\%) are small and mostly questions (31\% and 35\% not happy), so a ``travelling to X'' checklist and clearer port timelines are low-cost fixes rather than priorities.

\subsection{Insight huddle: operate the dashboard as an alert-and-review tool}

Do not publish a brand league table. A weekly 30-minute process is enough:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item flag not-happy posts in Network, App \& Account or Porting only when that week's volume clears a threshold set relative to a normal week (twice the recent median);
  \item open about ten source records and discard sarcasm, mixed-brand and off-target labels;
  \item triangulate with tickets, outages and billing;
  \item assign one owner and a response date;
  \item next week, check whether that issue's share fell.
\end{enumerate}

Use GPT-4o-mini for sentiment on the whole feed: labelling all 9,162 v2 posts cost about \$0.28, so a daily run costs cents. For issues, keep keyword rules and apply GPT-4o to \texttt{general\_discussion} rows. On hard posts, GPT-4o labelling every post is more accurate and, at about \$0.0005 per post, affordable on a deduplicated feed. Keep a person in the loop before any public reply. That mix is the business reading of the evaluation, not a model contest.

\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}

\subsection{Representativeness and platform bias}

Forum contributors are self-selecting and are not Singapore mobile users at large. Findings use the v2 corpus of 9,162 unique posts, after removing repeated copies (24,690 raw rows held 11,643 unique posts) and 96 off-topic threads. The corpus is 62\% Reddit, and 95\% of posts are from 2026; the few older posts are opening pages of long HWZ threads. Twelve per cent of posts sit in outage threads. Two collection artefacts are worth stating. The original HWZ scraper only ever read the first page of each thread because of a page-counting bug; wave~2 reads the newest pages instead. Wave-2 Reddit data come from the Arctic Shift research archive rather than Reddit directly, because Reddit blocks unauthenticated access and gates new API applications; the archive can miss content deleted before capture, and it includes replies that the original scraper skipped. There are no app-store reviews, tickets or NPS in the analysed set, so results cannot be generalised to market share, population sentiment, churn or Net Promoter Score.

\subsection{Unequal and dependent observations}

One record can mention several brands, so mention counts are not unique customers. Comments in a thread are not independent: they respond to a shared outage or launch. The report therefore shows thread-weighted shares beside per-post shares, and brand shares outside outage threads. Mixed-brand posts were often labelled \texttt{neutral}, which can hide a Singtel complaint sitting next to StarHub praise.

\subsection{Label quality and LLM misclassification}

GPT-4o-mini matches Annotator~A on about two-thirds of posts (accuracy 0.67, macro F1 0.631), and happy is its weakest class (F1 0.54). The benchmark is itself one annotator: on the representative overlap two humans agree only at $\kappa=0.38$, mostly because the guide did not say whether a bare outage report is \texttt{not\_happy} or \texttt{neutral}. Annotator~A treats such reports as complaints, and GPT-4o-mini agrees with A on the examples in Section~4, so part of its margin over VADER depends on that reading. All gold labels come from the original corpus: model accuracy on wave-2 content (reply chains, HWZ plan megathreads) is assumed, not measured, and a blind 100-post wave-2 gold sample (\texttt{wave2\_annotator\_a.csv}) has been drawn for that check. One of 9,162 v2 posts could not be labelled by GPT-4o-mini and falls back to VADER; 40 posts that failed in batches of ten were relabelled one at a time. A first wave-2 pass sent Reddit posts without their thread titles; those labels were discarded and the posts relabelled with titles. All-post LLMs under-label Devices relative to rules (GPT-4o recall 0.25 versus 0.81 on the representative set, 0.33 versus 0.75 on the challenge set) and often return one topic when the post has several. Keyword rules over-trigger on generic terms such as ``plan''. The hybrid's 0.680 was measured on the set used to choose it; on held-out hard posts its gain over rules was not significant and GPT-4o on every post did better. The corpus issue reassignments have no second human check. These errors are prompt- and snapshot-specific. Forum text sent to a vendor API is also a privacy limit: production jobs must not submit NRIC, phone numbers or other identifiers. The defensible stance is triage plus human review, not automated league tables.

\subsection{Privacy and responsible use}

Although forum posts are publicly accessible, public availability does not remove ethical obligations. Reports should avoid usernames and unnecessary verbatim quotations, minimise stored identifiers, restrict raw-data access and comply with platform terms. The system should analyse themes, not profile individuals. Credentials and API keys must be stored in environment variables or a secret manager and must never appear in code, reports or version control.

\section{Future Work}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Label the 100-post wave-2 gold sample and score GPT-4o-mini on it, to confirm that accuracy holds on wave-2 content.
  \item Relabel the double-annotation overlap under the new guide rule for first-hand failure reports, confirm that the second annotator labelled the challenge overlap independently, and adjudicate disagreements in a separate file.
  \item Compare the hybrid with GPT-4o labelling every post on a fresh held-out sample, since the two gold sets disagree on which is better.
  \item Spot-check the corpus rows that GPT-4o moved out of \texttt{general\_discussion}, especially the extra network-quality tags, before treating huddle counts as incidents.
  \item Add target-aware sentiment, so that a post praising one operator and criticising another yields a label per brand.
  \item Validate dashboard alerts against internal service tickets and network events before operational deployment.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

This project demonstrates a complete technical path from online forum collection to operator action. On 9,162 unique posts across six months, network quality dominates both the volume and the negativity of discussion, followed by plan terms and pricing. About two in five posts are not happy under every weighting; Reddit and HWZ differ mainly in topic rather than tone, and no brand stands out beyond small, weighting-sensitive differences, with Eight consistently the least negative. The original, outage-heavy corpus shows why this matters: one incident inflated negativity, made Singtel look uniquely negative and made Reddit look more negative than HWZ. The weekly huddle view flagged that outage and a later multi-brand bad week, and nothing else.

The analysis also shows why model governance matters. A perfect score from non-independent labels was discarded in favour of an imperfect but credible benchmark. On 295 blinded human labels, gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18 raises sentiment macro F1 from 0.360 (VADER) to 0.631; the gain holds on hard posts (0.773 versus 0.491), gpt-4o-2024-08-06 adds nothing for sentiment at about 17 times the cost, and repeated runs are stable. For issues the evidence is split: rules plus GPT-4o on unresolved posts is best on typical posts (0.680), and GPT-4o labelling every post is best on hard ones (0.596). The defensible deployment is LLM sentiment, rules plus a targeted LLM pass for issues, and human review of alerts, as an early-warning layer alongside, rather than in place of, customer-service and network data.

\section{Evaluation Checklist Before Submission}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Replace the title-page placeholders with the official student, course and supervisor details.
  \item Confirm the second annotator labelled the challenge overlap independently before relying on its $\kappa$; do not describe the labels as dual-adjudicated.
  \item Confirm that no credentials are included in the submission archive, Terminal history files or Git history.
  \item Remove or anonymise usernames and any unnecessary personal identifiers.
\end{itemize}
