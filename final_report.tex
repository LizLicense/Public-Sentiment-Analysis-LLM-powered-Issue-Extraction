\begin{abstract}
Singapore telecommunications operators receive continuous, unsolicited customer feedback through online forums, but the volume, informality and platform-specific language make manual monitoring difficult. This project develops an end-to-end pipeline that collects forum discussions, normalises heterogeneous records, identifies telecommunications brands, assigns sentiment and issue labels, and presents findings through a static HTML dashboard. The scraped corpus contains 15,504 rows from Reddit and HardwareZone (HWZ) but only 2,686 unique posts; after removing duplicates and 47 off-topic threads, the analysis corpus holds 1,706 posts in 105 telco threads. GPT-4o-mini labels 55\% of these posts not happy and 18\% happy, close to independent human labels on a gold set (50\% and 18\%), whereas VADER on the same posts reports 32\% and 40\%. Network quality is the largest issue theme per post and with threads weighted equally (53\% and 45\%), followed by mobile plans (12--13\%). Most negativity comes from a March 2026 Singtel outage: outside 22 outage threads the not-happy share falls to 42\%, an apparent Reddit--HWZ gap disappears (43\% versus 40\%), and Singtel's not-happy share (45\%) matches StarHub's (47\%).

The system combines deterministic brand matching, VADER sentiment, a keyword issue taxonomy, an optional Hugging Face sentiment model and OpenAI chat-completions classification. Independent human labels on a blinded 300-row representative sample (295 telco-relevant rows) show that \textbf{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18} improves sentiment over VADER (macro F1 0.631 versus 0.360; accuracy 67\% versus 37\%). \textbf{gpt-4o-2024-08-06} is similar on sentiment (macro F1 0.623) and still loses to keyword issue rules when it labels every row (macro F1 0.600 versus 0.639). A hybrid that keeps rule labels and applies GPT-4o only to unresolved \texttt{general\_discussion} rows raises issue macro F1 to \textbf{0.680}, and was applied to 8,786 unresolved corpus rows (29.7 minutes, estimated \textbf{\$3.45}). On a held-out 100-post challenge set of harder posts, the sentiment result holds (GPT-4o-mini 0.773 versus VADER 0.491), but the hybrid's gain over rules is not significant (0.509 versus 0.499) and GPT-4o labelling every post scores best (0.596), so no single issue method wins on both sets. GPT-4o-mini sentiment on the clean corpus cost \$0.047, and a repeated run reproduced 292 of 295 gold-set labels. An earlier 150-record VADER ``100\% accuracy'' file is discarded because its labels match VADER exactly and were model-prefilled. The operational recommendation is therefore LLM sentiment (mini is sufficient), keyword rules plus a targeted GPT-4o pass for issues, and human review of alerts. The business analysis indicates that operators should prioritise incident communication during outages, outage aftercare in billing and customer service, and comparable plan terms such as fair-use limits and add-on charges, ahead of app, roaming and porting issues, which are small in this corpus. The principal contribution is a reproducible analytics workflow that converts noisy forum data into decision-oriented signals while making its evidential limits explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Business context}

Mobile customers discuss price plans, coverage, roaming, porting and service failures in public online communities. These discussions can reveal emerging pain points earlier and in more detail than periodic surveys. They are also difficult to analyse reliably: posts contain Singlish, abbreviations, sarcasm, quoted text, multiple brands and off-topic uses of ambiguous brand names such as ``Eight'' or ``M1''.

This project asks how a telecommunications operator can convert such unstructured discussion into a repeatable source of customer insight. The objective is not to infer population-level satisfaction or replace operational metrics. Instead, the pipeline is designed as an early-warning and exploratory decision-support system that helps analysts locate themes, compare discussion contexts and inspect representative records.

\subsection{Objectives}

The project has five objectives:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item collect relevant discussions from multiple online platforms;
  \item clean and standardise heterogeneous post and comment records;
  \item detect mentioned operators and classify sentiment and service issues;
  \item compare lightweight baselines with more context-aware model options; and
  \item translate the results into actionable recommendations through a dashboard.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research questions}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0cm,style=nextline]
  \item[RQ1.] What sentiment and issue themes appear most frequently in the collected Singapore telco discussions?
  \item[RQ2.] How do discussion patterns differ between Reddit and HWZ, and across telco brands?
  \item[RQ3.] What additional value can a context-aware model provide over VADER sentiment and keyword-based issue rules?
  \item[RQ4.] How can the extracted signals support concrete decisions by telco operators?
\end{description}

\subsection{Scope and claimed contribution}

The repository implements collection modules for HWZ and Reddit. It includes preprocessing, brand detection, sentiment labelling, issue extraction, evaluation scripts and a static HTML dashboard. The report only claims findings supported by persisted repository outputs. GPT-4o-mini and GPT-4o are evaluated on human labels for both the representative and challenge sets, and GPT-4o is used on unresolved corpus issues. The findings and dashboard use GPT-4o-mini sentiment and hybrid issue labels on the 1,706-post clean corpus.

The scraped rows are not independent posts. The 15,504 rows hold 2,686 unique posts, because successive keyword rounds collected many posts several times (a median of four copies). Forty-seven of 152 threads were off-topic, such as a 98-post insurance lawsuit thread, and matched a keyword by accident. \texttt{processing/build\_clean\_corpus.py} keeps one row per post and drops a thread unless at least 20\% of its posts name a telco or its title is telco-related; kept threads name a telco in at least 25\% of their posts and dropped threads in at most 8\%. The resulting analysis corpus has 1,706 posts in 105 threads (1,253 Reddit, 453 HWZ). Every Reddit post is from 2026; HWZ posts almost all date from 2019--2025. An earlier 158-post dashboard sample (67 Reddit, 91 HWZ) is superseded.

\subsection{Preprocessing}

The preprocessing module maps platform-specific fields to a common schema, removes empty, placeholder and very short records, and can optionally retain English-language records using language detection. The common schema includes \texttt{source}, \texttt{thread\_title}, \texttt{post\_text}, timestamp fields and identifiers. Normalisation allows downstream modules to process different platforms without source-specific model code.

The current workflow retains post text that may include informal spelling, emoji and HTML entities because these features can carry sentiment. This is useful for analysis but also creates modelling challenges. The dashboard therefore presents aggregate indicators and traceable examples rather than treating model output as verified customer facts.

\section{Analytical Methods}

\subsection{Telco detection}

Brand mentions are identified by regular expressions over the concatenated thread title and post text. The tracked labels include Singtel, StarHub, M1, SIMBA, Giga, Eight, Zym and redONE, with additional MVNO keywords used by the dashboard. Multiple brands can be assigned to one record. This design is transparent and inexpensive, but ambiguous names and thread-level context can create false positives. For example, a post containing the ordinary word ``eight'' may be incorrectly associated with the Eight brand unless the surrounding context is considered.

\subsection{Sentiment baseline: VADER}

VADER is a lexicon- and rule-based sentiment method designed for social-media text \cite{hutto2014vader}. For each post, the pipeline obtains a compound score and maps it to:

\begin{equation}
\text{label}(s)=
\begin{cases}
\text{happy}, & s \geq 0.05,\\
\text{not\_happy}, & s \leq -0.05,\\
\text{neutral}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

VADER is a suitable operational baseline because it runs locally, is deterministic and requires no paid API. Its limitation is contextual understanding. It can misread sarcasm, negation over long passages, mixed opinions, quoted complaints and the difference between sentiment towards a telco and sentiment towards another topic in the same thread.

\subsection{Context-aware sentiment option}

The benchmark module supports \texttt{cardiffnlp/twitter-roberta-base-sentiment-latest}, a RoBERTa-family model adapted to social-media sentiment \cite{loureiro2022tweeteval}. Its positive, negative and neutral outputs are mapped to the project's three business labels. The persisted benchmark states that the required \texttt{transformers} package was unavailable, so no transformer metrics are reported.

The main labelling module also implements LLM sentiment through an OpenAI-compatible chat-completions interface. Its configured default is \textbf{GPT-4o-mini}. Formal comparison uses the snapshots returned by the API, \textbf{gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18} and \textbf{gpt-4o-2024-08-06}, with temperature 0 and JSON object responses in batches of ten posts. An earlier 100-record HWZ file contains 100 neutral LLM labels and no model-version metadata; that file is treated as a failed or fallback run and is not used as evidence. The clean corpus was labelled with the same prompt, batching and settings through \texttt{models/corpus\_llm\_sentiment.py}: 1,706 posts in 171 batches, no failures, 6.4 minutes and \$0.047.

The categories remain compact enough to route to owners while separating previously conflated topics such as plan design from promotions, billing from customer service, and app access from device problems. On the representative gold set that hybrid beats both keyword-only and all-row LLM tagging, so it was run on the 8,786 unresolved corpus rows. Section~\ref{sec:challenge} tests it on held-out posts.

\section{Evaluation Design and Evidence}

\subsection{Required metrics}

For a three-class sentiment task, overall accuracy alone can hide poor performance on minority classes. The evaluation module therefore calculates a confusion matrix and per-class precision, recall and F1, together with macro F1. For issue extraction, the appropriate measures are per-class precision, recall and F1 under a multi-label protocol, plus micro and macro averages.

Model comparison uses the same independently annotated records. Runtime is measured on the same machine, and the API run records input tokens, output tokens, retries, the exact returned model identifier and estimated cost from the provider list prices supplied at run time.

\subsection{Independent annotation}

A blinded evaluation package was generated without VADER, transformer or LLM columns. Threads were split so that representative and challenge posts do not share threads, with at most five comments per thread. Annotator~A labelled:

\begin{itemize}
  \item a 300-row representative sheet (214 Reddit, 86 HWZ), of which 295 posts were telco-relevant;
  \item a 100-row challenge sheet reserved for harder cases (negation, sarcasm or Singlish cues, mixed brands, weak VADER scores, long posts).
\end{itemize}

A second annotator labelled a 100-row overlap (60 representative, 40 challenge; \texttt{double\_annotation\_annotator\_b\_filled\_guide.csv}). On the representative overlap, sentiment agreement is 60\% (Cohen's $\kappa=0.38$ \cite{cohen1960kappa}, $n=58$), which is fair agreement at best; on the challenge overlap it is 75\% ($\kappa=0.63$, $n=40$). Most disagreements share one pattern: posts Annotator~A labelled \texttt{not\_happy} that Annotator~B labelled \texttt{neutral} (12 of 23 disagreements on the representative overlap, 8 of 10 on the challenge overlap). They are bare reports of a failure (``Ya Singtel is down''), questions about compensation, or troubleshooting advice. The annotation guide listed ``service failure'' under \texttt{not\_happy} and ``factual statement'' under \texttt{neutral} without saying which takes precedence, and such posts are both, so the two annotators resolved them differently. The guide now states that a first-hand failure report is \texttt{not\_happy}, which is Annotator~A's reading; the overlap labels predate that rule, and model scores against Annotator~A partly reflect it. Two further cautions apply. An earlier version of Annotator~B's sheet is superseded, because its sentiment labels often contradicted B's own notes and matched Annotator~A instead. And on the challenge overlap, 18 of 40 rows still match Annotator~A on every field, including four identical free-text notes, so the challenge $\kappa$ is not relied on until B's independence on those rows is confirmed. Headline metrics use Annotator~A's representative labels; challenge metrics are reported separately and are not pooled.

An earlier 150-row file reported 100\% VADER accuracy. Those labels match VADER exactly and can be created by prefilling from the model, so that file is treated as leakage and is not used as evidence.

The hybrid's gain over rules shrinks to 0.010 (CI $-0.039$ to 0.065), and GPT-4o labelling every post is best, beating both rules (0.097, CI 0.014 to 0.181) and the hybrid (0.087, CI 0.017 to 0.151). On these posts GPT-4o does better on customer service, roaming and billing, while rules still recall more device posts (0.75 versus 0.33). The hybrid remains the default for the corpus, because the representative set rather than the challenge set reflects its mix of posts, but its 0.680 should be read as optimistic. A union of rule and GPT-4o labels scored 0.703 on the representative set but 0.533 on the challenge set; it was found after seeing both results and is not adopted.

\subsection{Qualitative disagreements}

Examples are paraphrased from \texttt{qualitative\_disagreements.csv}. They illustrate why surface lexicons fail and why all-row LLMs drop device labels.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{REP-0003} (``SingTel eSIM still down''): human and LLM \texttt{not\_happy}; VADER \texttt{neutral}. Short status updates lack sentiment words.
  \item \textbf{REP-0014} (switched after 5G drops, cheaper 500\,GB plan): human and LLM \texttt{not\_happy}; VADER \texttt{happy} because of ``better'' and ``just \$12''.
  \item \textbf{CHL-0005} (``Smoooooooth as baby butttttt''): human and LLM \texttt{happy}; VADER \texttt{neutral}. Elongated slang is outside the lexicon.
  \item \textbf{CHL-0029} (nationwide outage, ready to port out): human and LLM \texttt{not\_happy}; VADER \texttt{happy}. Switching intent is a complaint, not praise.
  \item \textbf{REP-0015} (``Starhub, no ONR, no CGNAT''): human and rules label \texttt{devices}; GPT-4o-mini returns \texttt{general\_discussion} and flips sentiment to happy. Equipment jargon is a systematic all-row LLM miss; the unresolved hybrid keeps the rule \texttt{devices} tag on this class of post.
\end{itemize}

This section applies GPT-4o-mini sentiment and hybrid issue labels to the 1,706-post clean corpus. Shares are reported per post and with every thread weighted equally, so that one large thread cannot drive a finding on its own.

\subsection{Dashboard design}
\label{sec:dashboard}

\texttt{dashboard/generate\_dashboard.py} builds the static dashboard from the clean corpus. Chart titles are computed from the data and state the takeaway (for example, ``Network Quality leads, then Mobile Plans''), so the page cannot show a claim the current data no longer supports. It opens with a weekly 30-minute huddle playbook and a weekly queue chart against the 20-post threshold: (a) not-happy posts in Network Quality, App \& Account or Porting; (b) open ten source posts; (c) match them to tickets or outages; (d) assign one owner; (e) check next week whether the share fell.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tone:} GPT-4o-mini shares beside VADER and the human gold labels, so readers see why VADER is not used.
  \item \textbf{Source:} Reddit and HWZ on the same labeller, with the gold-label comparison.
  \item \textbf{Issue volume:} per-post and thread-weighted shares side by side; a finding that holds in both is not one thread talking.
  \item \textbf{Not-happy by issue:} which themes carry the negative discussion.
  \item \textbf{Brands:} only brands with at least ten posts are ranked, with thread counts in the table.
  \item \textbf{Recommendations:} ordered by each issue's share of not-happy discussion.
\end{itemize}

Outputs remain screening signals, not verified customer facts.

\section{Business Recommendations}

Each recommendation names an owner, an action and a check. They are ordered by each issue's share of not-happy discussion in the clean corpus. Forum counts are a triage queue, not proof of market failure.

\subsection{Network: communicate during incidents, then prove local fixes}

Network quality carries 52\% of not-happy discussion, and 412 of 599 not-happy network posts sit in outage threads (``Is Singtel mobile data down?'', ``SingTel is down for local subscribers, unless you have a Malaysian SIM''). The first action is incident communication: a status page and proactive updates with an estimated restoration time, posted where customers are already asking. Outside outages, 64 not-happy network posts name a place such as MRT tunnels, homes or offices; network teams should join those posts to tickets and cell data. Success is a shorter time to the first public update during an incident, and fewer tickets at a named location after remediation.

\subsection{Billing and customer service: plan the outage aftercare}

Billing (65\% not happy) and customer service (60\%) are small themes but the most negative, and many of their posts follow the outage. Customers ask whether compensation is a refund or a ``goodwill'' credit, object to a \$5 late-payment notice, and report help lines that were unreachable when needed. Service and billing teams should publish the compensation rule with the incident notice and keep a self-service path open during outages. Success is fewer compensation and ``cannot reach support'' posts after the next incident.

\subsection{Product and commercial: make plan terms comparable}

Mobile plans (12--13\% of posts) and pricing (50\% not happy) centre on terms customers discover late: fair-use limits on ``unlimited'' plans, add-on charges such as Caller ID (which posts note the three network operators charge for while SIMBA includes it), and introductory prices that later change. Product and commercial teams should show fair-use rules, add-on charges, recurring price and host network at checkout. Success is fewer ``which plan?'' and hidden-charge threads.

\subsection{Devices: document equipment and eSIM requirements}

Device posts (6\%) cover eSIM failures during the outage and ONR/bridge-mode router policy. Device support should publish router and eSIM requirements per plan. Success is fewer equipment threads.

\subsection{Lower-volume themes: app, roaming and porting}

App \& Account (1--3\%), roaming (2--4\%) and porting (3\%) are small in this corpus. App posts describe the app failing to launch on some Android phones after an update; digital teams should watch forum posts after each release. Roaming and porting posts are mostly questions (37\% and 23\% not happy), so a ``travelling to X'' checklist and clearer port timelines are low-cost fixes rather than priorities. The earlier sample-based priority on App \& Account is withdrawn.

\subsection{Insight huddle: operate the dashboard as an alert-and-review tool}

Do not publish a brand league table. A weekly 30-minute process is enough:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item flag not-happy posts in Network, App \& Account or Porting only when that week's volume clears a minimum (for example 20 posts);
  \item open about ten source records and discard sarcasm, mixed-brand and off-target labels;
  \item triangulate with tickets, outages and billing;
  \item assign one owner and a response date;
  \item next week, check whether that issue's share fell.
\end{enumerate}

Use GPT-4o-mini for sentiment on the whole feed: the 1,706-post corpus cost \$0.047, so a daily run costs cents. For issues, keep keyword rules and apply GPT-4o to \texttt{general\_discussion} rows. On hard posts, GPT-4o labelling every post is more accurate and, at about \$0.0005 per post, affordable on a deduplicated feed. Keep a person in the loop before any public reply. That mix is the business reading of the evaluation, not a model contest.

\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}

\subsection{Representativeness and platform bias}

Forum contributors are self-selecting and are not Singapore mobile users at large. The scraped corpus has 15,504 rows but 2,686 unique posts, and 47 of 152 threads were off-topic; findings use the 1,706-post clean corpus. That corpus is 73\% Reddit and concentrated in time: every Reddit post is from 2026, and 39\% of posts sit in 22 outage threads, mostly the March 2026 Singtel outage. The findings therefore describe a period dominated by one incident as much as the market. There are no app-store reviews, tickets or NPS in the analysed set, so results cannot be generalised to market share, population sentiment, churn or Net Promoter Score.

\subsection{Unequal and dependent observations}

One record can mention several brands, so mention counts are not unique customers. Comments in a thread are not independent: they respond to a shared outage or launch. A long Singtel downtime thread can dominate network counts without implying nationwide prevalence. The report therefore shows thread-weighted shares beside per-post shares, and brand shares outside outage threads. Mixed-brand posts were often labelled \texttt{neutral}, which can hide a Singtel complaint sitting next to StarHub praise.

\subsection{Label quality and LLM misclassification}

GPT-4o-mini matches Annotator~A on about two-thirds of posts (accuracy 0.67, macro F1 0.631), and happy is its weakest class (F1 0.54). The benchmark is itself one annotator: on the representative overlap two humans agree only at $\kappa=0.38$, mostly because the guide did not say whether a bare outage report is \texttt{not\_happy} or \texttt{neutral}. Annotator~A treats such reports as complaints, and GPT-4o-mini agrees with A on the examples in Section~4, so part of its margin over VADER depends on that reading. Corpus sentiment shares are therefore estimates, although they land close to the gold distribution. All-post LLMs under-label Devices relative to rules (GPT-4o recall 0.25 versus 0.81 on the representative set, 0.33 versus 0.75 on the challenge set) and often return one topic when the post has several. Keyword rules over-trigger on generic terms such as ``plan''. The hybrid's 0.680 was measured on the set used to choose it; on held-out hard posts its gain over rules was not significant and GPT-4o on every post did better. The 8,786 corpus reassignments have no second human check; the 2,080 extra network-quality tags in particular should be sampled before they drive a huddle. These errors are prompt- and snapshot-specific. Forum text sent to a vendor API is also a privacy limit: production jobs must not submit NRIC, phone numbers or other identifiers. The defensible stance is triage plus human review, not automated league tables.

\subsection{Privacy and responsible use}

Although forum posts are publicly accessible, public availability does not remove ethical obligations. Reports should avoid usernames and unnecessary verbatim quotations, minimise stored identifiers, restrict raw-data access and comply with platform terms. The system should analyse themes, not profile individuals. Credentials and API keys must be stored in environment variables or a secret manager and must never appear in code, reports or version control.

\section{Future Work}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Relabel the overlap under the new guide rule for first-hand failure reports, confirm that the second annotator labelled the challenge overlap independently, and adjudicate disagreements in a separate file.
  \item Compare the hybrid with GPT-4o labelling every post on a fresh held-out sample, since the two gold sets disagree on which is better.
  \item Spot-check the 2,606 corpus rows that GPT-4o moved out of \texttt{general\_discussion}, especially the extra network-quality tags, before treating huddle counts as incidents.
  \item Extend collection beyond the 2026 incident period, with more HWZ threads from 2024--2026 and more MVNO brands, and install \texttt{transformers} to run Twitter-RoBERTa on the same 295 records.
  \item Add target-aware sentiment, so that a post praising one operator and criticising another yields a label per brand.
  \item Validate dashboard alerts against internal service tickets and network events before operational deployment.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

This project demonstrates a complete technical path from online forum collection to operator action. On a clean corpus of 1,706 unique posts, network quality dominates both the volume and the negativity of discussion, driven largely by the March 2026 Singtel outage, and the weekly huddle view flags exactly those weeks. Billing and customer service are small but the most negative themes, and plan-term transparency is the main theme outside incidents. Outside the outage, Reddit and HWZ read alike and no brand stands out.

The analysis also shows why model governance matters. A perfect score from non-independent labels was discarded in favour of an imperfect but credible benchmark. On 295 blinded human labels, gpt-4o-mini-2024-07-18 raises sentiment macro F1 from 0.360 (VADER) to 0.631; the gain holds on hard posts (0.773 versus 0.491), gpt-4o-2024-08-06 adds nothing for sentiment at about 17 times the cost, and repeated runs are stable. For issues the evidence is split: rules plus GPT-4o on unresolved posts is best on typical posts (0.680), and GPT-4o labelling every post is best on hard ones (0.596). Relabelling the corpus residual cost \$3.45 and corpus sentiment \$0.047. The defensible deployment is LLM sentiment, rules plus a targeted LLM pass for issues, and human review of alerts, as an early-warning layer alongside, rather than in place of, customer-service and network data.
